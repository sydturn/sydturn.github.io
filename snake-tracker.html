Good signs — she's comfortable
👅
Regular tongue-flicking — the most reliable sign. She's curious and actively reading her environment. A tongue-flicking snake is an engaged snake.
🔍
Slow, flowing movement — exploring at her own pace, no urgency.
😌
Relaxed muscle tone — she feels loose and smooth, not tense or rigid.
🤲
Resting in your hands without constantly pushing off or escaping.
Neutral — tolerating it
〰️
Wriggly and restless — she'd rather be elsewhere but isn't panicking.
🙈
Heading for your sleeve or pocket — looking for somewhere dark and enclosed. Usually curiosity, not stress.
Stress signals — time to wrap up
🫥
Not tongue-flicking at all — a snake that goes still and stops tasting the air is often shutting down. Looks calm but isn't — this is a key one to learn.
😤
Hissing — a clear "I'm not happy" signal.
💨
Musking — releasing a smell from her vent. More serious stress response.
⚡
Striking — last resort. In hognoses this is almost always a closed-mouth bluff.
🪗 Hognose drama — don't be fooled
Western hognoses are infamous actors. Neck flattening (cobra impression), playing dead, and fake strikes are almost always theatrical bluffing — not genuine distress. Most hognoses grow out of it as they get used to handling. If she's doing all of this and musking, that's more meaningful.
Source: Hognose Snake Handling & Body Language — Reptifiles. The most thorough evidence-based hognose resource out there.
